No standard errors: this is the frontier logit's objective -- the empirical Pareto frontier P_t(c) sampled on the fixed cost-date grid -- minimised subject to the envelope's constraints: the surface must lie at or above every run (perfect scores clipped by their own sample size) and be monotone in cost and date. Grid nodes are not observations and the constrained fit has no likelihood, so errors would be fictions twice over. N is the nodes at which the frontier is defined. BC columns are a Box-Cox alternative to the quadratic: the index is linear in phi(cost), phi(time) and their product, with phi(x; lambda) = (x^lambda - 1)/lambda (log at lambda = 0) applied to LEVEL cost per task and to years since November 18, 2021, when the GPT-3 API became generally available -- so the BC intercept is the fit at $1 per task on November 18, 2022, where both transforms vanish. phi is increasing whatever lambda is, so the surface is monotone in cost at every date -- the quadratic's bending back toward the data cannot happen -- while the product term still allows the cost slope one sign change over time. These are BOX-TIDWELL fits: phi acts on the REGRESSORS only and the index is the plain logit, so no Jacobian arises. A response-side lambda on the odds was tried and removed -- a transformed response has to be inverted to be read back, and that inverse has a pole at a finite index which the profile walked into. The lambdas are estimated per benchmark by profiling the probability-scale quasi-likelihood, subject to the envelope's constraints, and are reported without standard errors: the profile provides none, and this model reports none anywhere. The BC specification nests the linear one (lambda_cost = 0, lambda_time = 1, no product term) but not the quadratic. fm13's five months of data sit 5.7-6.1 years from the origin, over which every lambda_time fits alike, so lambda_time is fixed at 1 there rather than estimated; elsewhere it is weakly identified and best read as a shape the data tolerates rather than demands -- a lambda_time whose profile runs to the edge of its search box ([-8, 8]) is treated as unidentified and locked at 1. This table has no summary column: the average column's content is the cost drop row, which only the linear specification carries.
